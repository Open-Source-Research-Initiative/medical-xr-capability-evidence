> Since 2020, has progress from commercial XR and AI capabilities to useful, sustainable medical applications accelerated—and what technical, staffing, financial and organizational conditions enable or constrain further acceleration?

The immediate objective is a small, human-reviewed demonstration of whether the required observations exist. The broad corpus can support an evidence map. Acceleration is a separate, conditional analysis. **Insufficient evidence to determine acceleration is an acceptable result.**

## Deliverable and boundaries

Prepare one worked medical-task family with a dated evidence table, source locators, a comparison ledger, an explicit account of unavailable observations, and a short human-reviewed interpretation. Attempt the whole chain: dated commercial capability → implemented hardware/software/AI configuration → observed medical-task outcome → resources and continued use. Distinguish failure to recover a link from evidence that the link did not occur.

The initial ceiling is one task family and up to six configuration/deployment histories, with all linked reports retained. This is a workload limit, not a statistical sample-size justification or an eligibility cutoff. If more qualifying histories are found, log them and stop for a documented scope decision rather than silently choosing the most successful six. A single complete chain demonstrates extractability for that case; it establishes neither a population rate nor acceleration.

Candidate families include a repeated training task, a planning/guidance task with a common error measure, or a repeated rehabilitation delivery task. These are options, not selected cases. Select one family before inspecting its outcome values, using task specificity, identifiable configurations, source access and plausible longitudinal reporting. Publish the selection rule, candidate list and prior exposure. Record unsuccessful and abandoned candidates. Keep the broader medical map and inherited eligibility scope unchanged.

## Three distinct analyses

### Performance progress within a task

Only compare observations meeting the frozen family rules. Orient improvement consistently and retain original units. An interval rate is `(y2-y1)/(t2-t1)`; three comparable dated observations permit two interval rates as a descriptive calculation. They do not, by themselves, establish acceleration: uncertainty, dependencies, changing populations/resources and measurement changes may explain the difference. Repeated reports of one configuration are not independent advances.

Report dates as intervals when necessary. Vary plausible event dates, exclude incompatible configurations, separate supplier from observed-study claims, and show the effect of dropping each dependent lineage. Do not interpolate missing outcomes or manufacture a common performance score. If comparable observations or their uncertainty are inadequate, report that limitation instead of a trend test.

### Transfer into implemented use

A series containing only published medical applications estimates intervals **conditional on observed published transfer**. It cannot estimate the transfer probability or speed of the technology population. Label any such summary accordingly.

A broader transfer analysis requires a separately defined risk set of capabilities plausibly relevant to the chosen task, assembled independently of whether a successful medical application was published. Record the applicability rule, availability date, geographic/access conditions, attempted implementations, discontinued attempts and ascertainment process. Seek registries, archived technical records, negative/unfinished implementations and institutional evidence; absence from a paper search is not evidence of non-transfer.

For each capability, distinguish an observed implementation event, an observed abandoned attempt, active surveillance without an event through a documented date, and unknown ascertainment. Only the active-surveillance case supports right censoring; a missing publication alone does not. Abandonment can be a competing event for an attempt, while the underlying capability may later transfer through a different product or organization. Company failure does not prove that technical value disappeared.

Use actual availability as the proposed time origin, retaining pre-2020 origins when relevant. Address entry after availability as delayed ascertainment/left truncation rather than resetting the clock. Recent technologies have shorter observation opportunity. Fix a common follow-up horizon before comparing eras; report numbers at risk and observation opportunity. A censored time-to-event or competing-risk model is conditional on a credible risk set and its assumptions, not a default repair for publication-only selection. If that frame cannot be built, do not claim field-level acceleration in transfer.

### Usefulness and sustainability

Keep technical performance, usefulness for a medical task and sustained delivery as separate claims. Record adverse outcomes and failed delivery as well as benefits. Education or workflow endpoints may support their own claims without proving patient benefit. Organizational conditions may be observed associations or qualitative explanations; this small worked analysis cannot establish their causal effects.

For sustainability, start follow-up at documented implementation and retain the last known use/support date. Compare only compatible follow-up horizons; distinguish persistence, observed cessation and missing follow-up. Record staff, funding, procurement, maintenance, access and transfer of a capability between organizations. An initial pilot or a plan for future use is not evidence of sustained use.

## Search coverage and contribution

The inherited search is identifiable, not demonstrably complete. First seek actual saved database histories, query logs, export records and dated correspondence through authorized access. A recovery record must identify the original artifact, its hash, what it establishes and what remains unknown; file timestamps are not reconstructed search dates.

If the follow-up queries/dates/filters cannot be recovered, choose explicitly between an inherited-corpus evidence map and a separately documented search update. For an update, an information specialist should review parallel clinical, technical and implementation/commercial-history streams, including interactive/nonimmersive and patient-specific 3D. Candidate sources include MEDLINE/PubMed, the relevant Web of Science collections, engineering indexes, registries, and archived supplier/institutional records; access and exact sources remain to be finalized. Avoid making AI, costs or a positive medical result mandatory retrieval concepts.

Freeze full syntax per database/platform/index, execution date/time, limits and reasons, result/export counts and caps, file hashes, deduplication/version linkage and citation-search seeds. Maintain an outside-inventory sentinel set chosen independently of screening decisions. Finding all sentinels is a diagnostic, not proof of complete recall. No search update was executed for this plan. [PRISMA-S reporting guidance](https://www.prisma-statement.org/prisma-search) supplies the search-documentation framework.

Adoption-barrier synthesis alone is not a demonstrated novelty claim. The supplied comparator, [Baltissen et al.'s 2026 review](https://www.jmir.org/2026/1/e80055/), includes 64 XR-rehabilitation papers and patients', clinicians' and developers' perspectives using NASSS. That overlap motivates a focused comparison; it does not establish that the whole Medical XR question is redundant. The prospective contribution is the source-supported linkage of dated capability, configuration, task outcome, resources and sustained use. Compare that joint linkage against existing reviews and their source studies before claiming novelty. This bounded comparator check is not an exhaustive novelty review.
