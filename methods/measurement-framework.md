# Measuring progress, usefulness and sustainability

Status: **13 November 2026 clarification draft for human scientific review.** It develops the inherited codebook; it is not a prespecified analysis, completed extraction or amendment to saved screening decisions. No acceleration result has been calculated.

The [proposed feasibility milestone](../docs/feasibility-milestone.md) makes the next decision concrete: one small human-reviewed chain and a comparison/ascertainment audit, with insufficient evidence to determine acceleration as an acceptable result. Its proposed analysis rules remain pending human review.

The interpretation unit is a dated capability/configuration performing a medical task under stated conditions. Link reports, versions, study populations and deployments before counting independent evidence. Keep technical performance, clinical usefulness and implementation separate.

| Construct | Proposed observation | Evidence needed | Insufficient evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Capability progress | Task performance, error, latency, reliability or feasible task set, with hardware/software/AI and conditions | Directional improvement on comparable tasks and measures, with uncertainty and resource changes | Product names, marketing specifications or publication counts alone |
| Transfer into use | Availability, research configuration, evaluation and implementation dates as separate events | Traceable capability-to-task pathway and dated intervals, with unknown dates qualified | Publication date treated as deployment date; availability assumed to prove access |
| Useful application | Clinical, educational, usability or workflow outcome appropriate to task; population, comparator, scale and follow-up stated | Benefit supported for the specific claim, including adverse effects and limits | Positive author wording or feasibility proving patient benefit; universal thresholds across unlike tasks |
| Sustainable application | Continued use, maintenance/support, training, staffing, delivery cost, funding and access over stated follow-up | Observed persistence and supported resource/organizational conditions; plans labelled planned | Absent follow-up treated as success/failure; company closure proving technical value vanished |
| Acceleration | Changing comparable rates or shortening comparable transfer intervals over time | At least three dated comparable observations permit two interval rates; stronger field-level inference needs more evidence and uncertainty | Two endpoints prove only change; paper or investment growth alone |

Candidate measures include task error per procedure, training performance on the same validated scale, setup/staff time per delivery, cost per delivery with stated cost boundary, and months between documented availability and implemented use. They are not interchangeable outcomes or mandatory eligibility gates. Clinical importance and acceptable performance need task-specific source justification and human review.

For a comparable metric y at times t, an interval rate is (y2−y1)/(t2−t1), retaining units and direction. Comparing successive rates requires comparable configurations, tasks, outcomes and time definitions. Transfer intervals need their own denominator and censoring treatment. Recent technologies have less follow-up opportunity; absent later reports are not observed non-adoption. No overall composite score or causal effect of calendar time is proposed.

Use the inherited evidence labels: OBSERVED_STUDY, SUPPLIER_REPORT, PAPER_REPORTED_PRODUCT, DOCUMENTED_LINEAGE, CALCULATED_ASSUMPTIONS, INTERPRETATION and UNKNOWN. Record claimant and inspected extent. Supplier claims do not become observed medical performance. Keep NR, NOT_MEASURED, UNCLEAR and NA distinct; missing access is not zero. Optional calculations or simulations need explicit inputs and assumptions and cannot replace observations.

Before analysis, a human methods review must fix comparison families, extraction fields, date rules, outcome directions, resource costs, follow-up, missingness and justified statistics. Record that decision and exposure to already-inspected results. Routine screening remains separate from deeper extraction.
