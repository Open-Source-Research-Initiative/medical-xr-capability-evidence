> Since 2020, has progress from commercial XR and AI capabilities to useful, sustainable medical applications accelerated—and what technical, staffing, financial and organizational conditions enable or constrain further acceleration?

**Accepted interpretation.** The main work is a retrospective, evidence-based analysis of enabling technologies and the capabilities they support. Identify hardware, software, actual AI functions, data and relevant expertise used in selected studies. Connect configurations to observed results and dated product specifications, prices and commercial evidence. Investigate whether task capability, performance, accessibility or affordability improved, whether progress became faster, and what documented changes help explain the pattern. A new experiment testing a generic list of adoption conditions is not the default design.

**Systems of systems.** Trace enabling components/platforms → clinician or educator working with technology → team and workflow → hospital or education institution → wider health-system capability and access. This is a map of relationships to investigate, not an assumption that benefits automatically propagate. For each connection, identify the task, operating conditions, inputs/outputs, responsible organizations, dependencies, measured result and source. Vendors, developers, clinicians, hospitals and funders can pursue different objectives and change their systems independently.

Capabilities may cross products, applications and organizations. A commercial initiative can be unsuccessful while leaving reusable components, algorithms, interfaces, software, manufacturing knowledge or expertise. Conversely, commercial success does not establish medical usefulness. Trace documented inheritance and availability, including migration costs or lost support. The user's Meta, supply-chain and robotics examples are investigation leads; current status, expenditure and causal contribution are not established by this decision.

**Progress and acceleration.** Compare task-relevant accuracy, reliability, failure/recovery, latency, preparation and verification effort, learning or clinical performance, feasible tasks, delivery cost and access. Keep task conditions, case difficulty, comparator, operator experience and evidence strength explicit. Supplier specifications describe product properties; they do not substitute for medical-task validation.

Distinguish capability improvement, transfer into medical workflows, and acceleration. Two isolated endpoints support a change comparison, not a claim of acceleration. Estimate rates only across sufficiently comparable dated evidence, with metric, scale, observation period and uncertainty stated. Recent projects have less follow-up; missing continuation does not prove failure. Report demonstrated capability, practical availability, actual use and distribution of access separately instead of creating an unsupported overall score for societal progress.

**Evidence and calculations.** Primary inputs are study methods/results, historical product/SDK documentation, release and support histories, contemporaneous prices, financial disclosures and documented operational experience. Preserve product/version, release, study, measurement and publication dates separately. Label statements as study observations, supplier reports, documented historical links, calculations under assumptions, interpretations or unknowns. Aggregate corporate spending cannot be assigned directly to a medical capability without a supported link.

Calculations or simulation are optional, targeted aids: cost per completed case, throughput using measured preparation/verification requirements, or sensitivity to latency and staffing assumptions. Use them when they resolve a specific question supported by the data. They are not mandatory deliverables; conditional outputs do not become observed clinical or financial results.

The broad medical map includes anatomy/procedural education, patient communication, surgical preparation/guidance, and rehearsal/mentoring/robotics; rehabilitation remains eligible.
